Living in Leander, Texas

Leander is a fast-growing suburb in Williamson County, about 20 miles northwest of Austin on the 183/183A corridor, roughly 20 minutes to the Domain and 35 to downtown. It runs about 10 to 20 percent cheaper than Austin, most of it is served by Leander ISD, and because it sits on the edge of the Hill Country, you can actually find homes with views. I grew up in Cedar Park just down the road, I move more clients to Leander every year than to any other north suburb of Austin, and I've filmed tours of 16 neighborhoods here so far.

What it's like to live in Leander

My honest read: Leander feels suburban. You'll find strip centers, national chains and master-planned communities, not dense walkable blocks. The town character lives in Old Leander, around City Hall and the historic downtown.

It's also growing fast. When I filmed my first city guide in August 2024, Leander had roughly 86,000 people and was growing about 10% a year. When I was a kid, Block House Creek was basically the only neighborhood out here. Now almost everything is new: Horizon Lake, for example, started building in 2018. Areas that felt like the middle of nowhere ten years ago now have an H-E-B, a Costco and new schools down the street.

There aren't many big employers in Leander itself, so most people commute to Austin or Cedar Park. That's where the MetroRail matters. If you work downtown, you can get on the train in Leander instead of fighting I-35 or 183. I've had clients choose Leander for that reason alone.

The big change is Northline, at 183 and San Gabriel Parkway. Two years ago it was only apartments and townhomes. When I filmed in September 2026, ground had broken on what's going to be Leander's new downtown: an open-air shopping district with a Sprouts under construction, restaurants, professional offices and brownstones like you'd find on the East Coast. They always build the residential first, because those residents become the customers that bring the businesses in.

Is Leander boring? It depends on your phase of life. If you're young and single and want to go out with friends, you'll probably still drive out of Leander for most of that. I'm married with two little girls, and we find plenty to do: parks like Lakewood Park and Horizon Lake, more local restaurants opening all the time, gyms and outdoor activities. It lives suburban, but Northline is changing that fast.

Leander vs Cedar Park vs Liberty Hill

This is one of the biggest questions my clients ask me. Here's the funny part: if you commute toward the Domain or downtown Austin, all three are basically 30 minutes from wherever you're going. 183 made the commute that easy. This is a general statement, but here's how they compare:

  • Liberty Hill is the northernmost and farthest out. It feels the most rural of the three.
  • Leander feels like a suburb, and almost everything here is new. If you want a newer home and don't mind driving into Austin for a night out, it's a great fit.
  • Cedar Park has exploded since Apple moved in, and it's more established. My parents bought the house I mostly grew up in brand new in 2002, and a lot of Cedar Park is around that age. You get bigger trees and easy shopping, but a higher price. You'll struggle to find new construction under $800,000 in Cedar Park these days.

If you're a younger family or a younger couple, Leander and Liberty Hill are both good places to look. Walk through a Cedar Park neighborhood and you're a lot more likely to see high schoolers than in Leander.

Leander ISD in 2026: the budget and school closures

For a lot of the people who reach out to me, Leander ISD is the reason they look here. It's one of the best-rated school districts in the country, and it covers all of Cedar Park and parts of Austin, not just Leander.

It's also going through real turmoil. As of September 2026, the district was about $18 million upside down heading into 2026-27 and was weighing closing or consolidating three elementary campuses; Faubion, Cypress and Steiner Ranch were named in the proposal. It has also opened enrollment to students from outside the district. That's caused a lot of uproar among parents. This is a live process, so confirm the current status with the district.

There are other good options too. BASIS and Valor both have highly rated public charter campuses in Leander, there are homeschool co-ops, and ACC has a campus here with some really interesting programs. Palmera Ridge's on-site elementary is not one of the campuses on the closure list.

How Leander is laid out

I think about Leander in four rough areas:

  • South Leander, near Cedar Park. Horizon Lake, Edgewood and Barksdale sit here, off the Ronald Reagan and Parmer Lane corridor. Apple is about 15 minutes down Parmer, and these neighborhoods live a lot like Cedar Park. Rouse High School serves part of this area.
  • Central and north Leander, along 183 and Lakeline Blvd. Carneros Ranch, Rosenbusch Ranch, Cedar Brook, Hawkes Landing, Hawkes Cove and South Brook are here. You're close to 1890 Ranch shopping (Costco, Target and restaurants) and to Divine Lake Park, a city park with a reservoir and miles of trails.
  • Northwest Leander acreage. Greatwood, Leander Estates, Woodland Estates and Hilltop Ranch are near Glenn High School and a short drive to Northline. This is where you find acre homesites that aren't all the way out in Liberty Hill.
  • The Ronald Reagan corridor. Palmera Ridge and Bar W Ranch are up here, with the newer Bar W Marketplace H-E-B nearby.

The main roads are 183 and the 183A toll road south toward the Domain, Ronald Reagan Blvd, and Lakeline Blvd, which is being extended in the northwest part of town as of 2026.

183A is the biggest thing that's changed Leander since my 2024 guide. The toll road now runs all the way up to Liberty Hill, about 16 miles of uninterrupted travel from northwest Austin. From most of Leander you can be at the Domain in about 20 minutes and downtown in about 35. The real trade-off is that you'll pay tolls every day, about $4.50 each way to the Domain as of September 2026, and that adds up. 183A itself rarely backs up; the slowdowns are the two bends in 183 in North Austin, the same spots that backed up when I was a kid. State troopers sit on this highway a lot, too. In October 2025 I drove from the Domain to South Brook in about 25 minutes on the toll road, off-peak and against traffic.

On schools: most of Leander is in Leander ISD, but not all of it. Bar W Ranch has a Leander address and is zoned to Liberty Hill ISD. Always check the zoning for the specific address.

Costs and taxes in Leander

Generally, Leander runs 10 to 20 percent less expensive than Austin, and prices and the number of homes selling have been fairly stable for the past couple of years after one of the biggest run-ups anywhere during the COVID-era market.

If you're relocating, know that Texas property taxes tend to be high because there's no state income tax. Leander falls right in the middle: the average was about 2.01% in September 2026, from about 1.65% in historic Leander up to 2.6% or more in some neighborhoods. Budget by tax rate, not just price. Two homes at the same price can carry very different payments.

These are the rates from my neighborhood tours, so note the dates:

  • About 2%: Horizon Lake (June 2024), Hawkes Landing (2.01%, July 2025), Cedar Brook (2.02%, June 2025), South Brook (2.02%, October 2025), Wildspring (June 2026) and Leander Estates (2.02%, August 2026).
  • About 2.3%: Greatwood (August 2024).
  • 2.4%: Palmera Ridge (May 2026).
  • 2.7%: Bar W Ranch (May 2026), one of the highest rates I know of around Austin.

HOA dues are mostly modest. On my tours they ran from about $40 a month (Horizon Lake, Hawkes Landing) to $53 (Carneros Ranch), $70 (Rosenbusch Ranch, Cedar Brook, Barksdale, Edgewood) and $75 (South Brook). Palmera Ridge was $150 a quarter in May 2026, and Greatwood was $450 a year in 2024.

One Leander rule: the city requires xeriscaping (rock in side and back yards) across all builders to cut back on watering.

Who Leander fits

Leander fits families who want Leander ISD, people who commute downtown and want the train, and buyers who want more house or more land for the money than they'd get closer in. It's also one of the few places near Austin where you can buy an acre homesite without going all the way to Liberty Hill.

It's not for everyone. It's suburban, most people commute, and tax rates range from about 2% to 2.7% depending on the neighborhood. Where you land inside Leander matters a lot, for your commute, your schools and your tax bill.

The MetroRail is the one thing no other Austin suburb besides Cedar Park can offer, and I've had clients pick Leander for that alone. The honest trade-off is that Leander feels suburban and most of the town commutes. But it's grown so fast in the past five years that a lot of neighborhoods that used to feel far out now have shopping, schools and better roads right down the street. Leander questions I get asked

Where is Leander, Texas?
In Williamson County, about 20 miles northwest of Austin on the 183/183A corridor, just north of Cedar Park. It's roughly 20 minutes to the Domain and 35 minutes to downtown.
Is Leander, Texas a good place to live?
For families who want newer homes, strong schools, about a 30-minute commute to north Austin and prices 10 to 20 percent below Austin, yes. For nightlife and walkable urban living, not yet, though Northline is changing that.
What is the property tax rate in Leander TX?
The average was about 2.01% in September 2026, from about 1.65% in historic Leander up to 2.6% or more in some neighborhoods. On my tours, Bar W Ranch was 2.7% in May 2026. Check the rate for the specific address.
Is Leander ISD closing schools?
As of September 2026, Leander ISD was about $18 million short for 2026-27 and was weighing closing or consolidating three elementary campuses; Faubion, Cypress and Steiner Ranch were named in the proposal. It's a live process, so confirm the current status with the district.
Leander vs Cedar Park: which is better?
Cedar Park is more established, with bigger trees and higher prices, and new construction under $800K is hard to find there. Leander is newer and cheaper, with a nearly identical commute to the Domain or downtown.
Can you get a home with a view in Leander?
Yes. It's Leander's most underrated feature, because it sits on the edge of the Hill Country. Travisso and Palmera Ridge both have real Hill Country views.
How much are the tolls from Leander to the Domain?
About $4.50 each way on 183A as of September 2026. The toll road now runs all the way to Liberty Hill, so budget for it if you commute daily.
Is Leander boring?
It depends on your phase of life. If you want a night out with friends, you'll probably still leave Leander. With young kids, there's plenty here already: parks like Lakewood and Horizon Lake, more restaurants opening all the time, and Northline on the way.
Does Leander have public transit to Austin?
Yes. Leander is on the CapMetro MetroRail commuter line, along with Cedar Park, which runs into downtown Austin.
What is Northline in Leander?
Leander's new downtown: an open-air mixed-use district at 183 and San Gabriel Parkway. As of September 2026 the apartments and townhomes were open, ground had broken on the commercial side, and a Sprouts was under construction alongside restaurants, offices and brownstones.
Where can I find acre lots in Leander?
Northwest Leander. Greatwood is the under-the-radar resale option, and Leander Estates, Woodland Estates and Hilltop Ranch are newer acre-homesite communities nearby.
Why do Leander homes have rock yards?
The City of Leander requires xeriscaping across all builders to cut back on residential watering. It's a Leander rule, not a builder choice.
