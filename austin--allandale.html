Allandale: Walkable, Quiet and One of Austin's Most Sought-After Neighborhoods

Allandale is an established Central Austin neighborhood between Burnet Road and MoPac, built from the 1940s on, with mature trees, no HOA and Shoal Creek running right through the middle. It's one of the most sought-after neighborhoods in Northwest Austin, and for really good reason. You can walk to H-E-B, but it still feels quiet and tucked back from the traffic.

Location and walkability

Allandale sits in Northwest / Central Austin, between Burnet Road and MoPac, with Shoal Creek running dead center through the neighborhood.

Literally all of your daily things can be walked to. H-E-B and Walmart are walkable, and P. Terry's is across Burnet. Fine dining like Bartlett's and Uchiko is a short drive away. But the streets themselves are quiet and tucked back. You're going to feel really urban with the quietness of a more suburban neighborhood.

Parks and trails

Northwest Park is the neighborhood's big park, with playgrounds, hike-and-bike trails, biking areas, a fishing pond, tennis and pickleball courts and a pool in the summer.

The Shoal Creek trail runs down toward the Austin Library and connects through Pease Park. Shoal Creek Boulevard also has a bike lane that takes you to downtown.

History and topography

Allandale started building in the 1940s and kept going for about 30 years. Most of the original homes were done by the '70s and '80s, so the trees are big and mature.

Compared with Brentwood and Crestview across Burnet, Allandale has more hills, especially near the creek. You get the same size trees, but the topography gives a lot of homes natural privacy. Lots also tend to be larger than in neighboring communities.

Schools

Allandale is in Austin ISD:

  • Gullett Elementary for most of the neighborhood (a section on the north edge is zoned to a different elementary, so check the address)
  • Lamar Middle School
  • McCallum High School, one of the most competitive high schools in Austin ISD

Taxes and HOA

As of my May 2024 tour, the tax rate was generally under 2%, varying a little by section. There's no HOA.

Homes and prices

You'll see a real mix: 1940s and '50s bungalows, remodeled '70s and '80s homes, originals, and knock-down rebuilds.

Austin's SF-3 zoning update now allows two individually deeded units on one lot, set up as a small condo regime. So you'll see A and B units at the same street address.

As of May 2024, prices ran from older homes needing some updating in the $500,000-$600,000 range up to large new builds a little over $3 million. On the tour I walked a new Studio Celeste build on Woodview Drive, a little over $3 million: 5 bedrooms plus an office and a loft, vaulted ceilings, full spray-foam insulation, an outdoor kitchen and a pool. Capturing a backyard like that and not having any neighbors is the ultimate luxury.

Who Allandale fits

Allandale fits people who want to walk to groceries and parks, want Austin ISD schools like McCallum, and want an established neighborhood with character and no HOA. It's also a good fit for people who want to renovate or build new on a larger lot.

The trade-off is price. This is one of the most in-demand parts of Austin, and older homes often need updating. It also tends to be a neighborhood that people don't really leave, so good homes don't come up often.
