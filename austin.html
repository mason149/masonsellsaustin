Austin Neighborhoods I've Toured, by Part of Town

Austin is a big, spread-out city where the part of town you pick shapes your commute, your school district and your tax rate as much as the house does. I was born and raised here, and I drive and film these neighborhoods myself so you can see what they're actually like before you tour. Here's how I think about the city, and every Austin neighborhood I've toured so far.

By Mason Bleasdell, REALTOR® with Mark Martin and Company  ·  Updated September 2026

What it's like to live in Austin

Austin has a little of everything: walkable older neighborhoods near downtown, master-planned communities with pools and trails, and brand-new construction on the edges of town. People moving from East or West Coast cities are often surprised by how suburban and spread out Texas is. The neighborhoods that bridge that gap, where you can walk to coffee or a park and still have a yard, are the ones my clients ask about most.

Parks and trails are a big part of life here: Walnut Creek Metro Park, the Shoal Creek trail, Zilker Park, the Onion Creek Greenbelt and more than 100 miles of trails around Circle C.

How Austin is laid out

I think of Austin in five parts:

  • North and Central: Older, walkable neighborhoods like Allandale and Brentwood/Crestview, plus the Domain area and the Parmer Lane / Tech Ridge corridor near Samsung, Apple and General Motors.
  • East: Mueller, Agave and Loyola, close to downtown, the airport and East Austin's restaurants and live music.
  • South: 78704 near downtown, then South Austin along Slaughter Lane and Manchaca, where a lot of new homes are detached site condos.
  • Southeast and Del Valle: Master-planned communities near the airport, Tesla and SH-130.
  • Southwest: Circle C, Legend Oaks and Sweetwater, off MoPac and toward the Hill Country.

School districts. An Austin address doesn't always mean Austin ISD. Cameron Place is Manor ISD, Foxfield is Pflugerville ISD, Easton Park and Addison are Del Valle ISD, and Sweetwater is Lake Travis ISD. Listing sites often show the nearest school, not the zoned one, so check the specific address.

Costs and taxes in Austin

Tax rates depend on city limits, MUDs and PIDs, and they change every year. A snapshot from my tours:

  • Lower end: Hills of Bear Creek at 1.6% (January 2025); Loyola, Legend Oaks, Messinger Village and Cascades at Onion Creek at about 1.8% (2024-2025).
  • Middle: Marble Creek Crossing at 2.0% and Cameron Place at 2.2%; Goodnight Ranch's north section at 2.34% and Evelyn at 2.4% (all July 2026).
  • Higher end: Easton Park at 2.57% as of March 2026, most of it a MUD tax.

New construction started in the high $200,000s at Stoney Chase (March 2026). Older Allandale homes needing updates were in the $500,000s-$600,000s, and new builds there ran over $3 million (May 2024).

Neighborhoods I've toured

North and Central Austin

  • Allandale is an established neighborhood between Burnet Road and MoPac with mature trees, no HOA and McCallum High.
  • Brentwood and Crestview are walkable older neighborhoods just north of downtown, with Little Deli, Violet Crown and a MetroRail station.
  • Wells Branch is a 1970s-80s North Austin community with a MUD-funded park system, a library and no HOA.
  • Foxfield is a small MileStone community right on Walnut Creek Metro Park, about 7 minutes from the Domain.
  • Pioneer Crossing is a Northeast Austin master-planned community with two amenity centers and an elementary school on site.
  • Pioneer Hill is a hilly D.R. Horton community off 290 and I-35 with a $45 HOA as of April 2024.
  • Cameron Place is Pulte new construction with an Austin 78754 address off Parmer, from the high $300s as of July 2026.
  • Evelyn is a new Trophy Signature and Century community next to Cameron Place, with a planned pool and sports courts.
  • 5th and West is a downtown all-residential condo tower within about five blocks of almost anything.

East Austin

  • Mueller is the master-planned community built on Austin's old airport, with parks, trails and walkable restaurants.
  • Agave is an East Austin community where InTown Homes builds colorful, contemporary homes, some with downtown views.
  • Loyola is a small MileStone community off 183 with a 1.8% tax rate as of January 2024.

South Austin

  • 78704 covers Travis Heights, Bouldin Creek, Zilker, Barton Hills, South Lamar and Galindo, the heart of South Austin culture.
  • Village at Cooper Lane is a small David Weekley site-condo community in 78745, close to Williams Elementary.
  • Messinger Village is a MileStone site-condo community off Slaughter and Manchaca near Moontower Saloon.
  • Cascades at Onion Creek is an M/I Homes community just east of I-35 with a pool and trails along Onion Creek.
  • Creekside at Estancia is a KB Home community just west of I-35 with bigger backyards and some downtown views.
  • Hills of Bear Creek is a MileStone community near Buda with public-style streets and larger homes.
  • Cooper Villas is a gated 2017-built townhome community with plenty of guest parking.
  • Skybridge Lofts is a condo complex off South Congress with private garages and an infinity-edge pool.

Southeast Austin and Del Valle

  • Goodnight Ranch is a master-planned community on Slaughter Lane with new walkable shops and Blazier Elementary across the street.
  • Marble Creek Crossing is an M/I Homes community with an Austin address, Del Valle ISD schools and a 2.0% tax rate as of July 2026.
  • Easton Park is a large master-planned community near the airport with pools, a gym and an H-E-B under construction.
  • Addison is a Brookfield Residential community near the airport and Tesla with two amenity centers.
  • Onion Creek is an established golf course community with an HOA of about $300 a year as of March 2024.
  • Stoney Chase is a KB Home community in Del Valle across from Circuit of the Americas.
  • Cloverleaf is a Brohn Homes community on the Buda line with homes from the low $300,000s in early 2025.

Southwest Austin

  • Circle C Ranch is a large master-planned community on MoPac with over 100 miles of trails and on-site schools.
  • Avana is Circle C's newest, southernmost section, with more greenbelts and more modern homes.
  • Legend Oaks is an established community just north of Circle C with two pools and a $55 HOA as of May 2024.
  • Sweetwater is a Hill Country master-planned community zoned to Lake Travis ISD, with views for miles.

Who Austin fits

Want walkability and character? Look at Central Austin, 78704 and East Austin, and expect higher prices and older homes. Want new construction under $500,000 with an Austin address? Look at the Parmer / Tech Ridge corridor and Southeast Austin, and check the school district and tax rate carefully. Want pools, trails and big amenities? Look at Circle C, Easton Park or Goodnight Ranch.

Austin questions I get asked

What are the best neighborhoods in Austin TX?
It depends on what you want. My clients who want walkability love Allandale, Brentwood/Crestview and 78704, and families who want amenities often look at Circle C, Easton Park and Goodnight Ranch.
Does an Austin address mean Austin ISD?
No. Cameron Place is Manor ISD, Foxfield is Pflugerville ISD, Easton Park and Addison are Del Valle ISD, and Sweetwater is Lake Travis ISD. Always check the specific address.
What is the property tax rate in Austin?
It varies by neighborhood. On my tours from 2024 to 2026 it ran from about 1.6% at Hills of Bear Creek to 2.57% at Easton Park, depending on city limits, MUDs and PIDs.
Where can I find new construction in Austin under $400,000?
Southeast Austin and Del Valle. As of 2026, Stoney Chase started in the high $200,000s and Marble Creek Crossing in the low $300,000s. Cameron Place in North Austin started in the high $300s as of July 2026.
What is a site condo in Austin?
A lot of South Austin new construction is detached homes that are condos by legal definition, on private roads that are fire lanes. You own your home and land, but street parking is limited.
Which Austin neighborhoods are walkable?
Allandale, Brentwood/Crestview, Mueller, 78704 neighborhoods like Travis Heights and Zilker, and newer spots like Foxfield and Goodnight Ranch, where you can walk to parks, coffee or groceries.
What part of Austin is close to Samsung, Apple and Tesla?
The Parmer Lane / Tech Ridge corridor in North and Northeast Austin is close to Samsung, Apple and General Motors. Southeast Austin, like Addison and Stoney Chase, is closest to Tesla via SH-130.
Are there Austin neighborhoods with no HOA?
Yes. Allandale and Wells Branch had no HOA as of my 2024 tours. Onion Creek's HOA was only about $300 a year as of March 2024.
What is the weather like in Austin?
February is usually the coldest month, and winter storms usually bring ice, not snow. March through May is the wettest stretch, and August highs are near 100.
Is traffic bad in Austin?
It can be. I-35 is usually the worst at rush hour. From Sweetwater, for example, I'd plan on at least 45 minutes downtown at rush hour and about 30 without traffic.
