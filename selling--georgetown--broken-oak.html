My listing plan

How I'd sell your Broken Oak home

The same plan for every listing, at every price point.

01

A game plan, not a spending spree

Before anything goes live, we walk the house together and plan the prep. My goal isn't to have you spend money. It's to present your home in the best way possible, and I'll be honest about what that takes.

02

Staging, at my cost

If your home is vacant, I stage it at my own cost, whether it's close to $1 million or $300,000. Occupied homes get small tweaks before marketing day.

03

A custom video tour

Every listing gets one, at every price point, made by my full-time videographer. The first half sells the Broken Oak lifestyle, from quarter-acre lots and quiet streets to the Georgetown Square about 15 minutes away. The second half covers the details we don't want buyers to miss, like the upgrades and maintenance you've done, especially the ones they can't see.

04

Sunset photography

My photographers shoot at sunset, the best light of the day. Your home stands out when buyers are scrolling hundreds of listings on Zillow, and even the interiors look better with the sunset in the windows.

05

Listing-day materials

A printed MLS sheet and a flyer are waiting in your home, with the specs, system ages and video highlights. A buyer who skipped the description still won't miss anything.

06

A launch to agents' inboxes

Good agents are busy. They aren't sitting on the MLS waiting for your house. The day we go live, a pre-launch email puts your home in front of them.

Why I invest in video for every listing

Photos show a house. Video shows what it's like to live there, and in Broken Oak the lifestyle sells a lot of the home. Almost every lot is about a quarter acre, the neighborhood is set away and quieter, and the Georgetown Square, with its courthouse, live music and farmers markets, is about 15 minutes away.

I never assume a buyer knows the area. The first thing buyers say when they pull into Broken Oak is that it feels rural, and many don't know a new HEB was under construction just up the street when I filmed in June 2025. So the first half of your video tells that story, and the second half covers your actual house: the upgrades, the lot, the systems and what's been updated.

It costs me real money to produce, with a full-time videographer, editing and staging. I spend it because a listing that gets watched gets toured, and I'm not afraid to invest in everything it takes to sell your home.

What you're up against in Broken Oak

Selling a resale in Broken Oak is different from selling in a big master-planned community. Here's what I plan around:

  • Builder competition. Chesmar and Highland Homes were both building when I filmed in June 2025, with a lot of customization. Buyers walk their models first, so your home needs to be presented at least that well.
  • The rural feel. Buyers notice it right away. I address it up front and show what's changing nearby, from the new HEB to the projects along Williams Drive.
  • Simple amenities. There's park space and trails but no resort pool. For a lot of buyers that's a plus, and I make sure they also know how Broken Oak's tax rate compares with Parmer Ranch and Nolina.

Honest pricing matters too. I'll show you what's actually selling in Broken Oak and price your home to sell, not just to get the listing.
